Take part

Take partLast reviewed 2026-07-312 min read

The nine pages you need before, during, and after you sign, from opening a world to completing a path, written for the moment money is involved.

This is the section where you stop reading and start doing. Every page here assumes you are about to sign a real Bitcoin transaction and pay a real fee, and it tells you exactly what you are agreeing to before you agree to it.

What taking part actually involves#

Joining a means signing one Bitcoin transaction. There is nothing to buy, no account to open, and no subscription.

You pay two things and no more. A network fee to miners, the same as any other Bitcoin transaction. And 1,000 satoshis held inside each output for as long as that path is alive, carried from step to step and released back to you when the path is completed.

Everything else is craft. Choosing the shape of a world. Writing an invitation people want to answer. Deciding what your one moment adds. Knowing when to stop.

▲Before you sign

A action cannot be undone. Not by the creator, not by us, not by you. The fee is paid whether or not you liked the result. Read the review screen every time, including the tenth time.

The nine pages#

Creating a world from start to finish The whole path: the idea, the settings you can never change, the invitation, funding, signing, and handing out the paths.

Joining and contributing to a world How to find a world, get a path, decide what to add, and sign your first step.

Returning to an existing path Three things to write down before you close the tab, and how to check where your path stands weeks later.

Completing a path What an ending does, why it is part of the work, and how it differs from letting a world run out.

Wallet connection and review Line by line through what you are asked to sign, and the lines that mean stop.

Fees and confirmation What you pay, what the 1,000 satoshis are doing, and what waiting for a block actually looks like.

Protect your path A path output is small. Ordinary wallets treat small outputs carelessly. Here is how not to lose one.

Follow and return Keeping track of a world you care about without an account, a login, or a notification.

Explore timelines Reading a world's history: the order of events, who answered whom, and where two paths met.

Where to begin#

If you have never done this, read Joining and contributing to a world first, then Fees and confirmation. Together they take about fifteen minutes and they cover the two things people regret not knowing.

If you are opening a world for other people, read Creating a world from start to finish. Several of its settings are fixed at the moment of creation and cannot be edited afterwards, so it is worth an hour of thought before it is worth a transaction.

◆Note

The public index that lets anyone browse confirmed worlds is not switched on yet, so there is no list of open worlds to scroll through today. What is running says exactly where things stand and what changes when it turns on.
