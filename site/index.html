How it works

Carry the thread forward.

Every valid event spends the current lane carrier and either creates its exact successor or closes the lane. Bitcoin orders the history; tiny canonical markers describe it.

  1. 01

    Create a world

    Choose a public seed, lifespan, step limit, and up to eight Taproot roots. The CREATE txid becomes the world ID.

  2. 02

    Move a carrier

    BLOOM and GRAFT add compact creative signals. The old carrier is input zero; its 1,000-sat successor is output one.

  3. 03

    Meet, then continue

    RENDEZVOUS links two confirmed lanes without merging them. Ordered inputs map to two ordered successors.

Security

The carrier is real Bitcoin.

The creative state is derived; the spend is not. A malformed or ordinary confirmed spend permanently abandons that lane. Isolate carrier UTXOs, verify every output, and wait for confirmation.

!

Experimental software. Start on regtest or signet. Mainnet use needs independent review and tested wallet controls.

Questions worth asking

Is ChainBloom a token or collectible marketplace?

No. CBLM v1 defines neither a token nor a transfer, listing, sale, royalty, reward, balance, or price primitive.

What does controlling a lane mean?

The key able to spend the current 1,000-sat Taproot carrier can advance or close that lane. ChainBloom does not create a separate legal or financial ownership claim.

Why are confirmed parents required?

Confirmations give independent indexers a shared order. Mempool events remain provisional, replaceable, and ineligible as canonical parents.

What happens after an invalid carrier spend?

The lane is abandoned. Bitcoin has no covenant enforcing a valid successor and ChainBloom has no administrator or recovery key.

Is mainnet use recommended today?

No blanket recommendation is made. The implementation is experimental; begin on regtest or signet and require independent review before risking mainnet funds.
